How much does fabric shrink? Cotton, linen, wool, viscose

Most natural and plant-based fibers shrink in the first wash, and the length usually shrinks more than the width. Prewash fabric the way you'll wash the finished garment, before you measure it and before you cut.

Typical shrinkage by fiber

These are common ranges for the first wash. The weave, the finish and the temperature all change them, so test your own piece when it matters.

  • Cotton, woven: often 2–5%. Flannel, gauze and loosely woven cottons can shrink more.
  • Linen: often 3–5%, sometimes more on loose weaves and in a hot dryer.
  • Viscose (rayon): often 3–10%, among the highest of common fibers, and it can distort. Wash it cool and dry it flat.
  • Wool: little in a cool, gentle wash; it can felt and shrink a lot in hot water with agitation. Many sewists steam it, or wash it the way they'll care for the garment.
  • Silk: varies a lot by weave, so test a piece; some silks change texture or lose their sheen when washed.
  • Polyester and nylon: close to none in normal washing.
  • Knits: cotton jersey often shrinks 3–5% in length, sometimes more, and can twist after washing. Blends with polyester or elastane shrink less.

What it means for a project: 5% of 2 m is 10 cm. That can be the difference between a hem and no hem.

Test a piece

  1. Cut a square, for example 25 × 25 cm, and mark which way the length runs.
  2. Wash and dry it the way you'll wash the garment.
  3. Measure both directions. Shrinkage (%) = (before − after) ÷ before × 100. A 25 cm side that comes out at 24 cm shrank 4%.

Prewash the whole piece

  • Wash it the way the garment will be washed. If you'll tumble dry the finished dress, tumble dry the fabric.
  • Finish raw edges with a zigzag or overlock, or the ends can fray away.
  • Press it before you measure and before you lay out the pattern.
  • Write the new length down. The stash length you planned with is the one before washing.
