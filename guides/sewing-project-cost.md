# How to work out what a sewing project cost

A handmade garment costs more than the fabric. Add up four things: the fabric you used, the notions, the pattern, and your time.

## 1. Fabric, by the length you used

Price the fabric by the length that went into the project, not the whole cut. If you bought 3 m for $36, it cost $12 a metre. A shirt that used 2.2 m used $26.40 of it; the rest is still in your stash, at the same price per metre.

If a fabric has no price written down, don't count it as free. Estimate it, or leave the total marked as incomplete.

## 2. Notions

Zips, buttons, thread, interfacing, elastic, bias binding. Count what went into this make. A spool of thread used across several projects can be split, or counted in full if that's simpler.

## 3. The pattern

Count its price, or a share of it if you'll sew it again. Printing a PDF pattern has a cost too (paper, ink, or copy-shop printing).

## 4. Your time

Log the hours as you go: cutting, sewing, fitting, pressing. Multiply by an hourly rate you choose. If you sew for yourself you can leave it out, but it's worth knowing the number.

## A worked example

- Fabric: 2.2 m at $12/m = $26.40
- Notions: zip $3, thread $2, buttons $4 = $9.00
- Pattern: $14.00
- Time: 6 hours at $15/hour = $90.00
- **Total: $139.40**

Without the hours, the shirt cost $49.40 in materials.

## Setting a sale price

- **Markup** is added on top of cost. A 50% markup on $139.40 gives $209.10.
- **Margin** is the share of the sale price that's profit. A 50% margin on $139.40 gives $278.80 (cost ÷ (1 − 0.5)).

Markup and margin at the same percentage give different prices, so pick one and use it every time.

Pleat, an iPhone app for people who sew, does this sum from your stash: fabric priced by the length used, notions, the pattern and logged hours, and it says when a fabric has no price instead of counting it as free. It can suggest a sale price from markup, margin, profit or an hourly rate. https://plts.app
