How to keep a fabric stash inventory

A stash inventory answers one question in the shop: do I already own something that would do? It only works if it's quick to keep up, so record a few things for every fabric and leave the rest for later.

What to record for each fabric

  • A photo. Daylight, the right side up, nothing else in the frame. You'll recognise the cloth faster from a picture than from a name.
  • Length. Measure along the selvage, not across. Write it in the unit your patterns use, metres or yards (1 yd = 0.914 m).
  • Fiber. Cotton, linen, wool, viscose, silk, polyester, or the blend if you know it. It decides what the fabric can be used for, and how it behaves in the wash.
  • Width. Usually 110–115 cm (45") or 140–150 cm (55–60"). Pattern yardage depends on it, so it matters more than it looks.
  • Weight. Light, medium or heavy is enough. If the shop gave you gsm (grams per square metre), keep the number.
  • Where it is. "Blue bin, top shelf" saves turning the cupboard out.
  • What you paid, if you want to know what a make cost later. Note whether it's the price per metre or for the whole cut.

Length, fiber and a photo are enough to start. Add the rest the next time you have the fabric in your hands.

Keep it current

  • Prewash before you measure, or note that it isn't prewashed yet. Cotton and linen can lose a few percent of their length in the first wash.
  • Update the length when you cut. An inventory that still lists fabric you've used is worse than none: you'll plan a project around it.
  • Mark fabric set aside for a project. It's still on the shelf, but it isn't free any more.
  • Keep scraps under a size limit out of the list. Pieces smaller than about half a metre are better in a scrap bin than in the inventory.

Spreadsheet or app

A spreadsheet works: one row per fabric, a column for each field above. It gets harder in the shop, where you need to compare a bolt in front of you with what's at home, and when several projects draw on the same piece.

Pleat is an iPhone app built for this: a photo and a length to add a fabric, a camera check in the shop against what you own, and fabric reserved for a project stops counting as free. It imports a spreadsheet as CSV. https://plts.app
